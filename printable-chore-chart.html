A printable chart that starts with your real routine

A useful chore chart is not the one with the most decorations. It is the one a child can read, remember, and use without a parent repeating every instruction. ChoreChartEasy gives you a clean Monday-to-Sunday grid, editable task names, large checkboxes, and age-based starting suggestions when you need help getting started.

Fastest path: open the blank chart, enter three to five chores, rename the chart, and print. If you have children of different ages, use the family generator so each child receives realistic responsibilities.

What to include on a weekly chore chart

  • Specific tasks: write “put toys in the basket” instead of “clean up.”
  • A visible finish: choose jobs a child can tell are done.
  • Age-appropriate responsibility: increase complexity gradually instead of assigning adult standards.
  • A small number of priorities: three to five recurring jobs are enough to establish the routine.
  • Shared family work: distinguish personal care from jobs that help everyone.

AgeGood starting chores

5–6Make the bed with help, clear a place setting, water a small plant, pack a school bag
7–9Set the table, fold simple laundry, sweep a small area, prepare a simple snack
10–12Load the dishwasher, vacuum a room, prepare lunch, manage personal laundry steps

Age is only a starting point. Consider motor skills, attention, safety, and how much practice the child has had. Demonstrate a task before adding it to the chart.

How to make the chart stick

  1. Choose a calm time to explain the chart.
  2. Practice each new task together before expecting independence.
  3. Put the chart where the routine happens—not hidden in an app.
  4. Let the child mark completed work.
  5. Review once a week and rotate only a few jobs at a time.
